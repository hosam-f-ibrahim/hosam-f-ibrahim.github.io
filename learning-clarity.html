Learning Clarity

Jan 2026 – Sep 2026 · intelliSYS Solutions & Consulting

A multilingual AI learning platform for web, iOS and Android that turns learners' notes, PDFs and documents into lessons, quizzes, timed exams and podcasts. I guided its architecture and product direction.

The problem

Learners usually study from their own material: lecture notes, slides, PDFs and documents, often in a language other than the one they think in. Rereading it is passive, generic courses don't follow it, and the people supporting them, teachers and parents, rarely see what has been understood.

What it does

A learner uploads material or describes a topic, and Learning Clarity finds the key subtopics and builds a course from it. Explain mode breaks the material into short lesson pages with knowledge checks; Quiz mode adapts its difficulty across four question types; Exam mode runs timed exams with score analytics and AI feedback.

Learning plans, analytics, achievements and certificates with public verification links turn the activity into measurable progress, and teachers and parents can follow it in real time, with weekly and monthly email reports.

The engineering behind it

  • Three apps, one platform. A TypeScript web app and Flutter apps for Android and iOS share one Supabase backend (Postgres, authentication, edge functions and scheduled jobs), so a learner's courses and progress follow them from laptop to phone.
  • AI grounded in the learner's material. The live AI features combine prompts, model calls and document retrieval, so lessons, quizzes, exams and answers come from what the learner uploaded, not from generic content.
  • One source, many forms. The same material becomes lesson pages, adaptive quizzes, timed exams, a two-voice podcast and a live voice conversation with a tutor that has read it.
  • Language separated from content. Learners can study their material and lessons in any language the AI models support, whatever language the interface is set to.
  • Private by design. Each account can reach only its own data, traffic is encrypted, and supervision is permission-based and can be revoked.

How I worked

As Head of Engineering, I guided the architecture and product direction for the team building Learning Clarity. For some of its live AI features, I directed a coding AI on the AI logic and reviewed its code or configuration; for others, I chose the AI design and reviewed the outcomes while the team handled implementation and testing.

That is the engineering judgment AI-assisted development now needs: deciding where AI writes the code and where people do, and keeping both aligned with the product and the architecture.
